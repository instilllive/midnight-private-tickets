The problem

Every ticket scan is a tracking event.

The venue sees too much

A scan ties your name, wallet or account to a seat and a time. Most doors only need one fact: this ticket is valid and unused.

Platforms build profiles

Ticketing and resale marketplaces link your purchases, your resales and every event you attend into one history.

On-chain tickets make it worse

An NFT ticket on a public chain is a permanent, linkable record: anyone can read which wallet went to which event.

Proving "I'm allowed in" should not require saying "I'm Mark, seat 14B".

Why privacy matters for events

Some events are sensitive by nature.

Who needs this

  • Private parties, member clubs and invite-only dinners
  • Support groups, health and community events
  • Political, activist and press events
  • Anyone who just doesn't want a lifelong attendance log

What the chain must not learn

  • Who you are (no name, no wallet address)
  • Which ticket is yours (the Merkle leaf stays hidden)
  • Who sold to whom on resale
  • That two scans came from the same person across events

Midnight's selective disclosure fits exactly: publish the minimum (a Merkle root and a one-time nullifier), keep everything else on the guest's machine.

Live demo summary

One scenario, every rule enforced.

1

Host issues tickets to Alice and Bob. Only hashes enter the tree.

2

Alice checks in with a ZK proof. IN · ledger learns a nullifier.

3

Second scan of Alice's ticket: REJECTED (nullifier repeats).

4

Stranger "Mallory" with no ticket: REJECTED.

5

Resale at $120 vs. $50 cap: REJECTED. At $45: OK, old ticket dies, buyer gets a fresh one.

6

Public ledger panel: hashes and nullifiers only. No names, addresses or ticket IDs.

Run it: npm run ui → host · guest · public-ledger panels on localhost:8799. Or npm run demo for the narrated terminal run. Video: demo/midnight-private-tickets.mp4 (phone · tablet · desktop).

What's new in this wave

A new Compact codebase for Midnight.

New this wave (Midnight)

  • Compact contract: issueTicket, checkIn, resellTicket
  • Merkle-membership proof with a one-time nullifier
  • Capped, unlinkable private resale with a per-ticket limit
  • 12-test suite and real proofs via proof server 9.0.0
  • Host / guest / public-ledger web UI, deck and video

Prior work, kept separate

  • Earlier Private Tickets apps on NEAR and Solana (Metaplex Core) share the idea, not code
  • Nothing from those was copied: Compact, the circuits and the proofs are written fresh
  • Private Tickets on Midnight is a new repo, MIT licensed

Honest status: the demo runs the compiled contract logic locally and real proofs via a local proof server. It is not yet deployed to a public Midnight network, and resale price is a rule check, not a payment. No mainnet use.

Business angle

Private-event hosts are the customer.

Who pays

Hosts of invite-only and sensitive events: clubs, communities, conferences, support groups. They want a guest list that cannot become a surveillance log.

How it earns

  • Per-event fee for issuing and door verification
  • Small fee on capped resales
  • Hosted door-scanner and analytics (aggregate only)

Why it wins

  • Hosts get fraud-proof check-in and anti-scalping
  • Guests get real privacy, a reason to choose these events
  • Midnight makes it possible without a trusted middleman

Go-to-market: start with small private events, learn from hosts, then add paid primary sales and shielded resale payments. (Hypothesis to validate; no revenue yet.)
